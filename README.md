# Supplementary material: melanins in *Bacillus*

Supplementary files for the Minireview

> Dagaldian, A. S., and K. A. Demin. "Melanins in *Bacillus*: Biosynthetic Pathways,
> Ecophysiological Functions, and Ecosystem Roles." *Applied and Environmental Microbiology*,
> manuscript AEM01927-26 (under review).

Academy of Biology and Medicine, Southern Federal University, Rostov-on-Don, Russia.
Corresponding author: Aleksandr S. Dagaldian, dagaldian@sfedu.ru

Two files are deposited here: the evidence table behind Figure 1 of the review, and a curated
catalogue of melanogenesis pathways and the enzymes involved in them.

## `Supplementary-file-1.docx` — melanin precursors and supporting evidence

A table of 14 candidate melanin precursors against six melanogenic model microorganisms
(*Aspergillus*, *Bacillus*, *Streptomyces*, *Exophiala*, *Sorangium*, *Azotobacter*), followed
by the reference list for every cell (28 references). This table is the source of Figure 1 of
the review.

Each precursor carries its metabolic origin: external substrates (L-tyrosine,
L-phenylalanine, L-tryptophan, caffeic acid), conditional external ones (L-DOPA), endogenous
intermediates of aromatic catabolism (4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate, homogentisate, catechol) and
endogenous intermediates of central or polyketide metabolism (acetyl-CoA, malonyl-CoA, T4HN,
scytalone, 1,8-DHN, HPQ).

Each cell states the strength of the available evidence:

- **D+** — direct culture-supplementation evidence: the compound was supplied exogenously and melanin production was confirmed.
- **I+** — endogenous-intermediate evidence: the compound was detected or biochemically established within a melanin-associated pathway in that organism.
- **G+** — genetic or pathway evidence: a relevant gene, expression study, mutation or functional pathway component supports the conversion, without direct supplementation data.
- **I+ G+** — both intermediate and genetic evidence for the same precursor and organism.

An empty cell means that no qualifying evidence was identified. It does not exclude the
conversion: the gap may reflect limited targeted investigation, the difficulty of detecting
short-lived intermediates, or genuine strain- and species-level variation within a genus.

## `Supplementary-file-2.xlsx` — catalogue of pathways and enzymes

Curated catalogue of melanogenic and competing aromatic-catabolism pathways, with emphasis on
*Bacillaceae*. Four sheets:

| Sheet | Content |
|---|---|
| `Legend` | Scope of the file, description of the other sheets, abbreviations and conventions. |
| `S1_Pathways` | Seventeen pathways (P01–P17): canonical DOPA eu- and pheomelanin, the *Bacillaceae* tyrosinase and CotA-dependent routes, pyomelanin and the alternative homogentisate routes, the competing catabolic branches, the fungal DHN and HPQ routes, and oxidative polymers from phenolic substrates. Each row gives the representative organisms, the strength of the evidence, the relevance to *Bacillaceae*, and the reaction sequence written out as alternating compounds and enzymes or processes. |
| `S2_Enzyme_index` | Forty-five enzymes and processes cited in `S1_Pathways`. For each: gene symbol, KEGG Orthology identifier, EC number, substrates, products, pathway context, role in the pigment phenotype (biosynthetic, precursor-forming, competing, and so on), cautions about the annotation, and interpretation for *Bacillaceae*. |
| `S3_References` | Full citations for the source identifiers (S01–S20) used in the "Source IDs" columns of the two other sheets. |

Conventions used in the file: NE marks a non-enzymatic reaction; "KO not assigned" means that
no robust KEGG Orthology entry exists for that enzyme; a pigment that has not been
structurally characterised is described as melanin-like rather than named.
